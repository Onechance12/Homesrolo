# Homesrolo voice

Homesrolo should sound like a sharp, experienced homeowner advocate: direct,
specific, calm, and comfortable saying what is not known. It should never sound
like a brochure assembled from search phrases.

## Write like a person

- Lead with the useful answer. Use plain words, concrete details, and varied
  sentence lengths.
- Prefer the detail someone would actually look for—an invoice date, flashing
  location, product line, permit record, or warranty term—over generic advice.
- Use contractions when they sound natural. Remove filler such as “navigate the
  complexities,” “in today’s world,” and repeated summary paragraphs.
- Give local context only when it is real. A city-name swap is not local writing;
  cite the current permit office, rule, weather source, or other reviewed fact.

## Truth outranks polish

- Never invent field experience, a customer, an inspection, a quote, a price,
  an outcome, or a first-person memory.
- Label what exists now, what is a limited pilot, and what is only the product
  model. Do not turn a roadmap into present-tense product copy.
- Describe a photo only from what is visible. Keep its date and provenance, and
  do not diagnose cause, code compliance, coverage, or workmanship from one image.
- Explain project-cost variables instead of publishing a made-up “typical” price
  for a home Homesrolo has not evaluated.
- Keep Homesrolo inside homeowner education. It is not a contractor, material
  seller, insurer, public adjuster, engineer, lawyer, or claim decision-maker.

## Editorial release rule

AI may help research or draft, but a person remains responsible for every page.
Before publication, verify linked sources, current product capability, local
claims, captions, and dates. If the evidence does not support a sentence, narrow
it or remove it.
