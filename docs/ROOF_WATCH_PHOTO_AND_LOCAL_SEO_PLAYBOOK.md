# Roof Watch photo and local SEO playbook

Roof Watch should grow from evidence gathered during real work, not from more
city-name copy. The strongest page is the one that can show what was observed,
who observed it, when the information was reviewed, and what the homeowner
actually receives.

## Before any field photo is published

- Confirm Homesrolo or the photographer owns the image and has written
  permission to publish it. A contractor's permission does not automatically
  cover a homeowner's property.
- Remove the street number, mail, license plates, faces, security systems, and
  other identifying details from the frame.
- Strip GPS and other unnecessary EXIF metadata from the exported web copy.
  Preserve an original in the private project record.
- Never imply that a photograph came from a Roof Watch visit unless it did.
  Stock or generated roof images are not inspection proof.
- Have the person who made the observation approve the caption. Do not infer a
  defect or storm cause from a photo alone.

## The first useful photo set

Capture ten to fifteen permission-cleared images before building a gallery:

1. Inspector at the property with identity and safety setup visible, but no
   homeowner or address.
2. Wide roof overview showing material and general geometry.
3. Valley with and without debris, from the same safe viewpoint.
4. Pipe boot or other penetration with a location reference.
5. Wall or chimney flashing detail.
6. Ridge, edge, and gutter condition.
7. Representative sound shingle area, not only defects.
8. A clearly bounded minor-maintenance before/after pair.
9. An example of an access limitation or no-walk condition.
10. Redacted inspection-report pages beside the corresponding field photos.

Add city pages only when the page can show real service evidence from that
area. One strong image with a useful caption is better than a generic gallery.

## File, alt-text, and caption pattern

Use a descriptive lowercase filename:

`keller-oak-debris-asphalt-roof-valley-2026-05.webp`

Write alt text for a person who cannot see the image:

`Oak leaves collected in an asphalt-shingle roof valley in Keller, Texas.`

Write a visible caption that adds context instead of repeating the alt text:

`Keller, TX — debris was holding moisture along this shaded valley. The visit
report marked the location for cleaning and comparison next year. Photo:
Homesrolo, May 2026.`

Do not put hashtags or a list of city keywords in filenames, alt text, or
captions. Hashtags have no special on-page search value. Accurate nearby text,
descriptive filenames, alt text, captions, dimensions, and crawlable image
markup do.

## Web export requirements

- Export the primary image as WebP or AVIF and retain a high-quality original.
- Set width and height to prevent layout shift.
- Use a normal `<img>` or Next `Image`; do not make important content a CSS
  background image.
- Keep the main page image at least 1,200 pixels wide when the source supports
  it. Do not upscale a small field photo.
- Provide a 1:1, 4:3, and 16:9 crop for article discovery when the composition
  can survive all three.
- Add the representative image to Open Graph, Twitter metadata, and the
  Article or Service structured data.
- Test the exported page with images disabled and with a narrow mobile viewport.

## Human editorial input for every article

Before a writer drafts a new Roof Watch guide, give them:

- a ten-minute interview with the person who did the field work;
- the approved field photos and plain descriptions of what each shows;
- three observations the professional sees repeatedly;
- one point they commonly disagree with and why;
- the exact service limits and scheduling process; and
- the primary sources for safety, legal, insurance, product, or weather claims.

Name the writer and field reviewer only with their permission. Avoid invented
customers, composite success stories, fake dialogue, and unsourced claims of
firsthand experience.

## Local SEO and links, in order

1. Fix truthful program terms, provider identity, and the Roof Watch signup
   path before sending more traffic.
2. Verify the domain in Google Search Console, submit the sitemap, and inspect
   the Roof Watch hub, guide pages, and every currently served city URL.
3. Publish a redacted sample report, the current inspection checklist, service
   limits, and a plain explanation of who performs the visit.
4. If Homesrolo meets Google's in-person business eligibility rules, complete
   one accurate service-area Business Profile. Do not use a virtual office or
   create a profile for each city.
5. Ask actual participating professionals to link to the exact program page
   from a page that identifies their role. Do not buy links or mass-trade them.
6. Earn local editorial links with useful material: anonymized condition
   summaries, homeowner workshops, a sample report, and resources for local
   inspectors, agents, HOAs, and chambers.
7. Expand to another city only after Roof Watch can really serve it and the
   page has local proof. Do not publish near-identical location pages.
